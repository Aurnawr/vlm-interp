LLaVA-1.5-7B results: refusal across text and image

1. t-SNE / PCA separation
- Harmful and harmless TEXT separate cleanly at every layer.
- Harmful and harmless IMAGES overlap a lot. There is only a partial split
  (harmful on one side of the image cloud, harmless on the other), never clean clusters.
- So the harmful/harmless signal is much weaker for images than for text.

2. Modality gap
- Image states and text states sit in completely separate regions at all layers
  (along PC1 in PCA). The model keeps "image input" and "text input" apart
  internally, even when the content is the same.

3. Refusal vector norms
- The text refusal vector is roughly 2-5x larger than the image one at every layer.
- The image norm barely grows until about layer 15. The text norm starts growing
  around layers 7-10.
- Both peak at layer 31 and drop at layer 32 (the final layer).
- This confirms that the image refusal direction is weak and shows up late.

4. Cosine similarity (text vs image refusal vector)
- Early layers (1-4): about 0 or negative, so the two directions are unrelated.
- Layers 5-15: flat at about 0.1-0.15.
- Layers 15-25: rises steadily to about 0.38, then stays at 0.38-0.45 up to layer 32.
- Even at best the similarity is about 0.45, so the image refusal direction only
  partly lines up with the text one.

Takeaway
- Images carry a weaker, later and only partly aligned harmfulness signal than text.
  This may explain why harmful images can get past refusal that was trained on text.
- Mid-to-late layers (about 20-31) are the best place to try steering or to compare
  the two refusal vectors, because that is where they align most.
