Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct results: refusal across text and image

Setup: same dataset as LLaVA (400 harmful AdvBench / 400 harmless Alpaca prompts, each
also rendered as an image with the neutral instruction "Respond to the request in the image.").
Last-token hidden state at every layer. 28 layers + embeddings, hidden size 3584.
Layer 0 is the shared last token of the chat template, so its refusal vector is zero (NaN rows).
Scripts: extract_hidden_states_qwen.py, plots_qwen.py, refusal_reliability.py --model qwen.

1. PCA separation
- Layers 6-11: the modality gap dominates (PC1 = 64-77% of variance). Text sits on one side,
  images on the other. Harmful vs harmless TEXT separate cleanly. Harmful vs harmless IMAGES
  form one tight blob with only a slight shift between them.
- Layer 17: all four groups are distinct. Harmful and harmless images now separate cleanly too.
  The modality gap is still there, but it has shrunk.
- Layers 22 and 28: the modality gap is gone. PC1 is harmful vs harmless, and text and image
  points of the same class lie on top of each other. The model represents a harmful image and a
  harmful text prompt in the same place.
- Held-out check (direction fit on even-indexed prompts, tested on odd-indexed ones):
  harmful vs harmless projected on the mean-difference direction is classified at
  text 0.93-0.96 / image 0.83-0.98 in layers 1-12, and 0.98-1.00 for both from layer 14 on.
- t-SNE (layers 6, 11, 17, 22, 28):
  - Layers 6-11: four groups. Text and images sit on opposite sides, and unlike PCA, t-SNE
    splits harmful from harmless IMAGES into two sub-clusters with only a small overlap. So the
    image harm signal is present early, just small next to the modality gap.
  - Layer 17: four clean, separate clusters, with no mixing between classes.
  - Layers 22 and 28: harmless text and harmless images merge into ONE cluster. Each harmless
    image sits right on top of its own text prompt. Harmful text and harmful images lie next to
    each other on the harmful side but form two adjacent sub-clusters (partly interleaved at 28).
- Image-to-text-twin check: for each image, is its nearest text point (out of all 800) the text
  version of the same prompt?
    layer   6    11    17    22    28
    harmless 0.01 0.02 0.61 0.94 0.98
    harmful  0.00 0.00 0.08 0.40 0.74
  So in late layers Qwen maps most images onto the representation of their own text, prompt by
  prompt. This is more complete for harmless than for harmful prompts. The harmful image and
  text centroids also stay further apart (distance 56-66 vs 30-41 for harmless, layers 22-28).

2. Refusal vector norms
- Early and middle layers: text norm is 2-5x the image norm (4.4x at layer 1, 2.3x at layer 14).
- The ratio falls steadily. From layer 19 on the two norms are nearly equal (ratio 1.05-1.16),
  and at layer 28 the image norm is slightly larger (0.97).
- Relative to the typical hidden-state norm at each layer, the refusal vector reaches about
  0.6-0.7 of the hidden-state norm for BOTH modalities in layers 20-26. So harmful vs harmless is
  a dominant feature of the late residual stream for both modalities.
- Both norms grow sharply after layer 18 (text 53 -> 395 by layer 27), then drop at the final
  layer 28 (text 161, image 166), as LLaVA drops at its final layer.
- Bootstrap 95% CIs on both norm curves are narrow (reliability/norms_with_ci.png).

3. Cosine similarity (text vs image refusal vector), with 95% bootstrap CIs
- Layers 1-13: 0.15-0.43. Already above 0 at layer 1 (0.23 [0.20, 0.25]).
- Layer 13 -> 14: a jump from 0.43 to 0.68 [0.66, 0.69].
- Layers 14-19: rises to 0.86.
- Layers 20-28: 0.90-0.95. Peak 0.952 [0.950, 0.953] at layers 25-26.

4. Is the cosine a real result? (reliability/reliability_table.txt)
- Noise ceiling (split-half self-consistency scaled up to n=400 with Spearman-Brown):
  text 0.99-0.998, image 0.99-0.999. Both refusal vectors are almost noise-free.
- Noise-corrected cosine (cross-modal / sqrt of the two self-consistencies) is within 0.005 of
  the raw cosine at every layer. Sampling noise explains none of the gap from 1.
- Random-direction null (95th pct |cos|): 0.031-0.034. Every layer is far above it.
- Bootstrap CIs are tight: at most +-0.03 early, +-0.002 in late layers.
- Massive-activation check: dropping the 10 largest-magnitude dimensions barely changes the
  cosine (0.946 -> 0.944 at layer 24), and no single dimension holds more than 9% of either
  vector's squared norm. The high late-layer cosine is spread across many dimensions, not driven
  by a few outlier dimensions.
- So you can write: "cos = 0.95 [0.950, 0.953] at layer 25, against a text self-consistency
  of 0.998 and an image self-consistency of 0.998." Late-layer text and image refusal
  directions in Qwen are nearly the same direction.

Takeaway
- In Qwen2.5-VL, the image harmfulness signal starts weaker than the text one but converges
  onto it. From about two-thirds depth (layer 19-20), images and text share essentially one
  refusal direction with equal strength, and the modality gap in PCA disappears.
- The transition is sharp, around layer 13-14 (halfway through the network).
- Layers 20-27 are the natural place to test one shared refusal direction, e.g. steer
  image inputs with the text-derived vector.

Caveats
- The images are typographic: the request is rendered text. Qwen2.5-VL is trained heavily on
  OCR, so late-layer convergence may mean "Qwen reads the text, then processes it as text".
  The image-to-text-twin result (98% at layer 28 for harmless prompts) points strongly this way.
  That is not the same as a modality-general harm concept. Natural harmful images are needed to
  tell these apart.
- The held-out separability, relative norms, outlier-dimension and image-to-text-twin numbers
  were computed ad hoc from the cached hidden states. No saved script produces them yet.
- Harmful (AdvBench) and harmless (Alpaca) prompts also differ in style and length. A stable
  direction is not necessarily a refusal direction. The random null is a weak floor. A
  label-shuffle null and causal tests (ablate/add the vector, measure refusal rates) are still
  missing.
- Refusal behaviour itself (does Qwen actually refuse the harmful images?) was not measured.
