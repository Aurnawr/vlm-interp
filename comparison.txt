LLaVA-1.5-7B vs Qwen2.5-VL-7B: refusal across text and image

Both models: same 400 harmful (AdvBench) / 400 harmless (Alpaca) prompts, as text and as
rendered images with the neutral instruction "Respond to the request in the image.".
Refusal vector = mean(harmful) - mean(harmless) of the last-token hidden state, per layer.
Details: llava-results/results.txt, qwen-results/results.txt, */reliability/reliability_table.txt.

Architectures differ, so compare by RELATIVE DEPTH (layer / number of layers):
  LLaVA-1.5-7B : CLIP ViT-L + MLP projector bolted onto Vicuna-7B, 32 layers, dim 4096
  Qwen2.5-VL-7B: vision encoder + LLM trained together on large multimodal/OCR data,
                 28 layers, dim 3584

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Alignment of the text and image refusal directions
- LLaVA: the directions stay mostly different. The cosine is 0.1-0.15 through the middle
  of the network, then rises to 0.38-0.45 at the end, well below a ceiling of 0.99.
- Qwen: the directions converge. The cosine is 0.15-0.43 up to mid-depth, jumps at
  layer 13 -> 14 (0.43 -> 0.68), and reaches 0.90-0.95 from layer 20 on.
- Both results are solid. The noise ceilings are ~0.99, CIs are tight, and the random null
  is ~0.03. So the difference between the models is real, not sampling noise.
- Early layers differ in sign. LLaVA layer 1 is significantly NEGATIVE (-0.28); Qwen layer 1
  is positive (0.23).

2. Strength of the image signal (refusal vector norms)
- LLaVA: the text norm is 2-9x the image norm at every layer. The image norm barely grows
  before mid-depth. Relative to the typical hidden-state norm, the late-layer image refusal
  vector is ~0.2 against ~0.5-0.6 for text.
- Qwen: the text norm starts 2-5x larger, but the ratio shrinks to ~1.05 from layer 19 on.
  Relative to the hidden-state norm, both reach ~0.6-0.7 in layers 20-26. Late in the network,
  images drive the harmful/harmless feature as strongly as text does.
- Both models show a norm drop at the final layer.

3. Geometry (PCA and t-SNE)
- LLaVA: the modality gap persists at every layer, up to layer 32. Text and images occupy
  separate regions. In t-SNE at layer 32, images form their own region made of many small
  mixed harmful/harmless clumps, well away from both text clusters.
- Qwen: the modality gap dominates early (PC1 = 64-77% at layers 6-11), shrinks by layer 17,
  and is gone by layers 22-28. There PC1 is harmful vs harmless. In t-SNE, harmless images
  and harmless text merge into one cluster; harmful images and harmful text sit side by side
  as two adjacent sub-clusters.

4. Does each image land on its own text? (image-to-text-twin check)
  For each image: is its nearest text point (out of all 800) the text of the same prompt?

   LLaVA layer   8     16    24    32     | Qwen layer   6     11    17    22    28
   harmless     0.00  0.01  0.06  0.06    | harmless    0.01  0.02  0.61  0.94  0.98
   harmful      0.00  0.00  0.07  0.14    | harmful     0.00  0.00  0.08  0.40  0.74

- LLaVA never maps an image onto its own text. Qwen does so for almost every harmless prompt
  and most harmful ones by its last layers. The convergence in Qwen is per-prompt, not just
  class-level, and it is weaker for harmful prompts than harmless ones.

5. Linear separability of harmful vs harmless images
  (held-out: mean-difference direction fit on even prompts, tested on odd prompts)
- LLaVA image: 0.68-0.86 in layers 1-4, 0.88-0.96 from layer 6 on (text: 0.92-0.98).
- Qwen image:  0.83-0.85 in layers 1-4, 0.94-0.98 in layers 6-12, 0.99-1.00 from layer 14 on
  (text: 0.93-1.00).
- Note: even in LLaVA the image classes are fairly separable along this direction. The PCA plots
  under-sell it because the modality gap takes up the top PCs. So LLaVA's image signal is not
  absent. It is weaker and points in a different direction from the text signal.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Interpretation
- In LLaVA-1.5 (adapter-style VLM), images carry their own weaker harmfulness signal that
  only partly aligns with the text refusal direction. This fits the idea that text-trained
  refusal does not fully transfer to images, which is a plausible route for image-based jailbreaks.
- In Qwen2.5-VL (natively trained VLM), the image signal is folded into the SAME
  representation as text by about two-thirds depth: same direction (cos 0.95), same strength,
  no modality gap. A single refusal direction should work for both modalities in layers ~20-27.
- Caution: both datasets use typographic images (the prompt rendered as text). Qwen's strong
  OCR training may simply let it read the image into a text-like representation. The
  convergence may be "OCR works well" rather than "harm is represented modality-independently".
  The image-to-text-twin result (section 4) points strongly toward the OCR reading.
  Natural harmful images are needed to separate these.

Caveats (both models)
- AdvBench vs Alpaca differ in style and length as well as harmfulness. A stable
  mean-difference direction is not proof it is refusal.
- Missing: label-shuffle null, causal tests (ablate/add the vector and measure refusal rates),
  and measured refusal behaviour on the image inputs.
- The per-layer relative norms, held-out separability, outlier-dimension and image-to-text-twin
  checks quoted here were computed ad hoc from the cached hidden states. No saved script
  produces them yet.
